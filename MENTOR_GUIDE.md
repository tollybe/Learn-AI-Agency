# Mentor guide

The mentor's job is to create the conditions for independent problem-solving. It is not to become a human help desk.

## The mentoring promise

Tell the learner:

> I will help you define the next useful challenge, choose safe boundaries, and review your reasoning. I will not remove every productive difficulty. You will leave with a repeatable way to solve the next problem without me.

## Diagnose the certification-to-capability gap

Someone may know terminology and still lack execution practice. Ask for demonstrations across six dimensions:

| Dimension | Diagnostic request |
| --- | --- |
| Concept | “Explain the idea without course language.” |
| Tool use | “Make the smallest example run.” |
| Judgment | “Compare two approaches under a real constraint.” |
| Execution | “Turn this brief into a usable artifact.” |
| Verification | “Show how you know it works and where it fails.” |
| Transfer | “Adapt or teach the pattern in a new setting.” |

Do not weaponise the gap. “Not demonstrated yet” is a project-selection tool, not a verdict on intelligence.

## Give tools, checkpoints, and boundaries

A strong assignment includes:

- a real user or use case;
- a small definition of done;
- constraints, including privacy and budget;
- one checkpoint before the learner overbuilds;
- required evidence;
- room for the learner to choose the implementation.

Avoid giving a full course and hoping practical confidence emerges later.

## Use the autonomy ladder

Move up or down based on evidence—not confidence alone.

1. **Model:** mentor demonstrates and narrates one example.
2. **Pair:** learner drives; mentor asks questions.
3. **Prompt:** learner attempts; mentor gives a narrow hint.
4. **Review:** learner submits an artifact and reasoning.
5. **Teach:** learner explains or supports another learner.

If the learner asks for rescue immediately, ask for their attempt-first note. If the cost of failure is high, step in sooner and make the safety boundary explicit.

## Review pull requests as mentoring checkpoints

Review in this order:

1. Does it solve the stated need?
2. Is there evidence it was tested?
3. Can the learner explain the important choices?
4. Are privacy, security, accessibility, and cost handled proportionately?
5. What is the single highest-value improvement?

Avoid turning every pull request into a professional code-quality audit. A learner needs a clear next move more than twenty comments.

## When the learner says “I need more experience”

Translate the statement into an experience you can manufacture:

- Instead of “take another Python course,” assign a script that removes a real repetitive task.
- Instead of “learn agents,” build a one-tool workflow, observe its failure modes, then decide whether an agent is justified.
- Instead of “learn Azure AI,” compare one cloud implementation with a free/local implementation and explain when the managed service earns its cost.

Experience is a sequence of increasingly independent decisions with feedback.

## A useful mentor response pattern

```text
You have enough background to attempt this.

Show me:
1. the outcome you want;
2. two approaches AI helped you find;
3. the smallest experiment you ran;
4. what happened;
5. the decision you now need help making.

I will review that decision with you.
```

This communicates confidence while keeping support concrete.
